## Deterministic model and failure contract

For sample `i`, the transparent serialized-beat reference is:

```text
a_i = (i-1) T_s
q   = A + b/r
s_i = max(a_i, f_(i-1))
f_i = s_i + q.
```

The baseline owns 32 deterministic source codes `mod(37(i-1)+11,4096)`, a 50 us request period, 2
byte/sample, 0.2 byte/us payload rate, 1 us/beat arbitration, eight samples per completion block, 8 us DMA
setup demand, 3 us completion-handler demand, and 4 us/sample counterfactual processor-copy demand. One beat
costs 11 us; offered load is 22%; completions are `11:50:1561 us`; block completions are
`[361 761 1161 1561] us`; block-ready latency repeats `[361 311 261 211 161 111 61 11] us`; DMA CPU
demand is 20 us versus 128 us processor-copy demand; and incrementing memory exactly equals the source.

Sweep 1 changes only block size `[1 2 4 8 16 32]`. Interrupts are `[32 16 8 4 2 1]`; DMA CPU demand is
`[104 56 32 20 14 11] us`; mean block-ready latency is `[11 36 86 186 386 786] us`; maximum is
`[11 61 161 361 761 1561] us`; beat timing, 352 us bus service, and destination data stay fixed.

Sweep 2 resets the block size and changes only payload rate `[0.025 0.04 0.05 0.1 0.2] byte/us`. Beat
service becomes `[81 51 41 21 11] us`; offered load becomes `[162 102 82 42 22]%`; maximum request latency
is `[1042 82 41 21 11] us`; peak pending count is `[13 2 1 1 1]`; descriptor completion is
`[2592 1632 1591 1571 1561] us`; and DMA CPU demand remains 20 us. Backlog is visible under an unbounded
finite source queue and is not labeled as data loss.

The broken case changes only destination increment to fixed addressing. All 32 beats and four completion
interrupts retain baseline timing, but slot one ends with code 1158, 31 writes overwrite it, 31 destination
slots remain unwritten, and data integrity is false. Completion therefore does not certify intended memory
placement.

For a final short block, `e_j=min(jB,N)` and `F_j=f_(e_j)`. The retained 10-sample, eight-sample-block
behavioral reference hands off at samples 8 and 10, completes those blocks at `[361 461] us`, assigns tail
ready latencies `[61 11] us`, charges two completion handlers for 14 us total DMA CPU demand, and preserves
destination integrity. The MATLAB executable check retains the corresponding five-sample fixture with
block endpoints `[3 5]`, completion times `[23 43] us`, ready latencies `[23 13 3 13 3] us`, and 2 us of
handler demand.

The timeout uses strict `descriptor_completion > timeout`; equality at 1561 us passes. At 1161 us,
`continue-waiting` records timeout and commits all 32 samples, while `abort-transfer` retains 24 samples and
three complete blocks. The equal-work observed CPU comparison is 96 us of committed processor-copy demand
versus 17 us of observed DMA setup/handler demand, not the 128 us full-transfer copy reference. At 1160 us,
only 23 samples and two complete blocks are visible; 253 us of completed-beat service plus 10 us on the
in-flight beat yields 263 us observed bus activity without fabricating a committed sample. The UI masks
canceled finishes and future starts while retaining that one pre-abort start. Clean state-free re-execution
after broken, overloaded, timeout, abort, malformed, and resource cases must reproduce the exact baseline.
This computational recovery is not DMA, peripheral, driver, memory, cache, downstream, or plant rollback.

The model validates before large allocation, accepts at most 10,000 source samples and a 1,000,000-sample
block declaration, restricts sample widths to 1–16 bytes, and bounds derived time at `1e12 us`. It rejects
malformed types and policies, nonfinite derived timing or CPU demand, oversized resources, and floating-point
collapse of a positive request interval or beat service. Row/column vectors, integer numeric classes through
`flintmax`, character/scalar-string policies, a final short block, and empty timeout/action/address defaults
are retained compatibility cases. Larger `uint64` or `int64` codes are rejected before double normalization
can silently alias source identity.

- inputs: source code (count), request period (us), sample width (byte/sample), payload rate (byte/us),

- timeline: sample index, request/start/completion, queue wait, request latency, and request budget (us), plus
  pending and completed counts;
- block: start/end sample index, actual block sample count, completion (us), ready latency (us), completion
  ownership, and observed/canceled readiness;
- CPU: cumulative and total counterfactual processor-copy and DMA control demand (us), plus signed service

- memory: planned/committed destination slot, committed/canceled/overwritten masks, source/destination code,
  written/unwritten/overwrite counts, and integrity flag;
- aggregate: bytes requested/committed/canceled, beat and payload service (us), request slack (us), offered bus
  load (%), bus-active time (us), peak pending requests, budget excesses, descriptor completion (us), block
  completion/interrupt count, ready latency (us), conservation residuals, and explicit no-loss/no-P14 flags.

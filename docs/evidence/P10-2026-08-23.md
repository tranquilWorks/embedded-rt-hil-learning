## Deterministic model and failure contract

- The default fixture has task IDs Low/Medium/High, releases `[0 1 2] ms`, pre-critical service
  `[0 5 0] ms`, mutex-held service `[4 0 1] ms`, post-critical service `[2 0 1] ms`, relative deadlines
  `[20 20 6] ms`, base priorities `[1 2 3]`, 1 ms ticks, and a half-open 16 ms horizon.
- Larger effective priority runs first; ties use earliest release and then task ID. A blocked waiter is not
  ready. With direct priority inheritance, the one mutex owner receives the greatest base priority among
  current waiters. Dispatch and context-switch overhead are modeled as zero.
- Boundary order is prior-slot completion/unlock, deadline classification, wait-timeout classification,
  half-open release, bounded request/block/reselection, then one tick of CPU service. Completion exactly at a
  deadline passes. Prior-slot unlock at the exact timeout boundary wins because it has already made the waiter
  ready. Deadline classification precedes cancellation when both expire at one boundary.
- With inheritance, CPU ownership is
  `[1 2 1 1 1 3 3 2 2 2 2 1 1 0 0 0]`; completion is `[13 11 7] ms`; High waits 3 ms of direct
  blocking, receives no third-party interference, responds in 5 ms, and meets its 8 ms absolute deadline.
  Low's effective priority is 3 for exactly the three intervals in which High is blocked.
- With a plain mutex, CPU ownership is
  `[1 2 2 2 2 2 1 1 1 3 3 1 1 0 0 0]`; completion is `[13 6 11] ms`; High waits 7 ms, decomposed
  as 3 ms direct owner service plus 4 ms unrelated Medium service, responds in 9 ms, and misses once at 8 ms.
- The Medium-work sweep `[1 3 5 7] ms` preserves releases, mutex work, deadlines, priorities, tick, horizon,
  timeout, and total work outside the lever. Plain-mutex High wait is `[3 5 7 9] ms`, response is
  `[5 7 9 11] ms`, and third-party delay is `[0 2 4 6] ms`; inheritance holds them at 3, 5, and 0 ms.
- The Low critical-share sweep `[1 2 3 4 5] ms` preserves six total Low work ticks, Medium work, releases,
  deadlines, priorities, and inheritance. High direct wait is `[0 1 2 3 4] ms` and response is
  `[2 3 4 5 6] ms`, showing that inheritance removes unrelated delay but not necessary owner service.
- A 2 ms wait timeout classifies High at 4 ms. `continue-waiting` retains donation and eventual completion.
  `cancel-waiter` synchronously removes High's two unserved ticks, withdraws donation, leaves Low owning the
  mutex until 9 ms, and conserves 13 ms as 11 served plus 2 canceled. A clean call recovers exact baseline
  state. This is modeled work removal, not asynchronous RTOS task deletion or physical rollback.
- Limiting cases cover High acquiring first, zero Medium work, one Low critical tick, zero-work completion,
  equal-priority ties, exact deadline, one-tick-late failure, release at the horizon, and a truncated horizon.
- The fixture contains one-shot jobs and therefore defines no periodic utilization metric. Protocol comparisons
  instead preserve released demand, task releases, base priorities, total service, and the observation horizon.
- Strict validation rejects malformed arity/type/shape/value/policy/grid inputs, tiny positives that would
  collapse to zero ticks, more than 16 tasks, more than 100,000 observation ticks, and individual declared
  times beyond 1e9 ticks. Accepted maximum fixtures remain compatible with the model.

  priority is separated from temporary effective-priority donation.

  conservation, malformed-input isolation, and explicit resource ceilings are retained.

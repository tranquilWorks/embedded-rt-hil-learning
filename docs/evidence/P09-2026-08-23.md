## Deterministic model and failure contract

- Baseline task inputs are periods `[5 10 20] ms`, WCET `[1 2 5] ms`, relative deadlines `[5 10 20] ms`,
  priorities `[3 2 1]`, phases `[0 0 0] ms`, 1 ms ticks, a 40 ms half-open horizon, and `continue-late`.
- Each release `r_i,k=phase_i+kT_i` creates a distinct FIFO job with absolute deadline `d_i,k=r_i,k+D_i`.
  Larger priority wins; equal priorities use earliest release, then task ID, then job sequence.
- Boundary order is prior completion, one-shot deadline expiration/cancellation, release, then dispatch for the
  next half-open interval. Completion exactly at a deadline is on time; releases at the horizon are excluded.
- The owner sequence repeats
  `1 2 2 3 3 1 3 3 3 0 1 2 2 0 0 1 0 0 0 0` twice. Releases are `[8 4 2]`, service is
  `[8 8 10] ms`, busy/idle are 26/14 ms, utilization is 0.65, worst response is `[1 3 9] ms`, misses are
  `[0 0 0]`, and logger is preempted twice.
- Independent fixed-priority recurrence `R_i(next)=C_i+sum ceil(R_i/T_h)C_h` over higher-priority tasks gives
  `[1 3 9] ms` for the clean task set.
- Priority sweep `[3 2 1]`, `[2 3 1]`, `[3 1 2]` keeps timing, releases, demand, horizon, utilization, and
  zero misses fixed while worst response becomes `[1 3 9]`, `[3 2 9]`, `[1 9 7] ms`.
- Logger-WCET sweep `[2 5 8] ms` resets priority and retains other inputs. Utilization becomes
  `[0.50 0.65 0.80]`; logger worst response becomes `[5 9 15] ms`; higher-priority response remains
  `[1 3] ms`.
- Broken priorities `[2 1 3]` retain the 0.65 utilization and releases but produce worst responses
  `[6 9 5] ms` and misses `[2 0 0]`, disproving that sub-unity utilization makes every priority map safe.
- `T=5,C=D=5 ms` completes exactly on time. With `C=6`, `continue-late` marks timeout at 5 ms and completes at
  6 ms; `cancel-at-deadline` records 5 ms service and cancels one modeled millisecond before dispatch.
- Calls are state-free. Restoring priority or deadline policy is exact computational rollback; a clean
  re-invocation is recovery. Cancellation is a synchronous model policy, not an asynchronous RTOS API or
  rollback of mutex, peripheral, or plant effects.
- Tasks are capped at 16, observation at 100,000 ticks, releases at 10,000 jobs, and individual declared times
  at 1e9 ticks. Bounds are checked before schedule/job allocation.
- The built-in scenario helper derives release count before returning. With periods `[5 10 20] ms`, 28,570 ms
  releases exactly 10,000 jobs and remains model-compatible; 28,571 ms releases 10,002 jobs and is rejected.
